# Worked example: illustrative consulting-team competency framework

Legend: Basic (negligible/basic exposure) / Foundational (led a
workstream) / Strong (led projects, holds relevant accreditation) /
Expert (leads numerous significant projects, recognized authority)

| Discipline | Basic | Foundational | Strong | Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Client management | Attends client meetings, minimal independent contact | Manages day-to-day client relationship on one workstream | Manages the overall client relationship on a project independently | Trusted advisor across multiple client relationships, brought in for the hardest conversations |
| Business analysis / problem-solving | Supports analysis under close direction | Has independently structured and analyzed a defined problem | Leads problem structuring on ambiguous, high-stakes questions | Sets the analytical approach across a program, coaches others in it |
| Change management | Basic understanding, no independent delivery | Delivered change activities (e.g. training sessions) under a change lead | Has led a change workstream end to end, holds relevant accreditation | Designs change strategy for large, complex, multi-release programs |

### How this gets used
- **Hiring**: candidates are assessed against the Foundational bar for
  the discipline most central to the role they're hiring for, not every
  discipline equally.
- **Development conversations**: quarterly 1:1s reference specific
  cells — "you're Foundational on change management; the gap to Strong
  is leading a workstream end to end, not just delivering activities
  under someone else's lead" — concrete enough to set a real development
  goal against.
- **Promotion criteria**: progression to the next title requires Strong
  in at least two disciplines, not just tenure — makes the promotion bar
  explicit rather than a judgment call with no shared reference point.
