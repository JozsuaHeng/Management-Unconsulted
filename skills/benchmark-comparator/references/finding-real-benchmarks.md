# Finding real benchmarks, practically

## When a formal industry report exists
Tourism boards, hospitality associations, and market research firms
sometimes publish aggregate statistics (average booking values, occupancy
rates, review score distributions). Cite the specific report and date —
these figures also age, so note how current the source is.

## When no formal report exists (common for niche categories like
multi-day boat charters in a specific region)

Construct a rough range from multiple public data points rather than
inventing a single number:
- Pull the same metric (e.g. listed response-time commitment, if stated)
  from 3-5 comparable operators' public information
- Present it as a range, not a false-precision average ("comparable
  operators publicly commit to response times of roughly 1-24 hours,
  with most around same-day" — not "industry average is 6.2 hours")
- Label this clearly as a constructed, directional estimate — moderate or
  low confidence, not a cited fact

## When genuinely nothing usable is found

Say so directly: "I couldn't find a reliable benchmark for this — here's
what I'd need to construct one (e.g. named comparable businesses willing
to share numbers, or a paid industry report), or here's a reasonable
internal target to set instead, based on [some other logic, e.g. what
would meaningfully improve conversion]." Setting an internal target
instead of a fake external benchmark is often more useful than pretending
a benchmark exists when it doesn't.

## Worked example: inquiry response time

Our number: average 6 hours (from internal booking system logs — high
confidence, directly measured).

Benchmark: checked 4 comparable Lombok-area charter operators' publicly
stated response commitments (where listed) — commitments ranged from
"within 1 hour" to "within 24 hours," with 3 of 4 stating same-day
response. Constructed estimate, moderate confidence (based on stated
commitments, not verified actual performance).

Comparison: our actual average (6 hours) is within the same-day range
most competitors commit to, though on the slower end of it.

So what: not an urgent gap versus competitors as stated, but worth noting
this compares to competitors' *stated commitments*, not their *actual*
performance, which is unverifiable — so this benchmark is directional,
not a precise ranking.
