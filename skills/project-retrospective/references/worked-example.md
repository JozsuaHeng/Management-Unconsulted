# Worked example: GIC booking system rollout retrospective

### Context
Retrospective on the 6-week booking system replacement project, held 2
weeks after go-live.

### What worked
- Hands-on training with shadowed practice (Ability stage) meant staff
  were comfortable within days, not weeks.
- Daily check-ins in week 1 caught small issues before they became
  habits.

### What didn't work
- One staff member missed the initial training session and wasn't
  caught until the readiness check a week later. Root cause (5-Whys):
  training was scheduled around one calendar, not checked against both
  staff members' actual availability — a scheduling process gap, not an
  individual's fault.
- The old spreadsheet was still being used for one edge case (group
  bookings over 10 people) three weeks post-launch. Root cause: the new
  platform's group-booking flow wasn't covered in training, since it's
  used rarely — an actual capability gap, not a habit-reversion issue.

### Recommendations for next time
| Recommendation | Owner | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Confirm training attendance against both staff members' calendars before scheduling, not just one | Owner | Next system rollout |
| Explicitly cover edge-case workflows (not just the common path) in training, even if rare | Owner | Next system rollout |
| Extend the readiness check to 3 weeks post-launch, not 1, to catch low-frequency workflow gaps | Owner | Ongoing process |

Note the group-booking finding: it looked like habit-reversion at first
glance, but the root cause was a genuine training gap, not backsliding —
worth the extra step of checking before assuming the obvious explanation.
