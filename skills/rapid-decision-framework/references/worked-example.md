# Worked example: deciding whether to launch the Gili route

| Role | Held by | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Recommend | Ops lead | Brings the scouting findings and unit economics |
| Agree | — (none needed) | Business is small enough that no separate sign-off layer is needed beyond the decider |
| Perform | Ops lead + booking staff | Execute the launch once decided |
| Input | Booking staff (customer sentiment from past inquiries) | Consulted but doesn't hold sign-off |
| Decide | Owner | Sole final call |

Note the deliberately empty "Agree" row — for a two-person operation,
inventing a sign-off layer just to fill out the framework would add
friction with no real governance benefit. RAPID scales down as
legitimately as it scales up; the discipline that matters here is still
having exactly one clear "Decide" holder, not padding out every role.
