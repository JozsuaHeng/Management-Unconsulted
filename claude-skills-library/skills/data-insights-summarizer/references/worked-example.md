# Worked example: illustrative post-trip customer survey (n=48)

**Headline finding**: Guests rate the crew experience highly, but
value-for-money perception is the clearest area holding back repeat
bookings — not trip quality itself.

### Supporting findings
- Crew friendliness/knowledge rated 4.7/5 average — Fact (direct survey
  average, n=48)
- Value-for-money rated 3.6/5, the lowest-scoring category — Fact
- This gap likely reflects price positioning relative to a specific
  named competitor mentioned in 9 of 48 open-text responses —
  Interpretation (a pattern in open text, not a directly asked question)
- Repeat-booking intent correlates more with the value-for-money score
  than with the overall trip-rating score in this sample — Interpretation
  (an observed pattern in a modest sample, not a proven causal driver)

### Data quality notes
n=48 is a moderate sample for a small operator, but too small to treat
sub-group breakdowns (e.g. by trip type) as reliable — those would need
a larger sample before acting on confidently.

### What this doesn't tell us
The survey doesn't establish *why* value-for-money scores are lower —
whether it's actual price level, unclear pricing communication, or
comparison to a specific competitor is inferred from open-text comments,
not directly measured. A targeted follow-up question would be needed to
confirm before making a pricing decision based on this alone.
