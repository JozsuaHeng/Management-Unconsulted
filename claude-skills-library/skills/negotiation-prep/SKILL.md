# Negotiation Prep

## When to use this

An actual negotiation is coming up — a contract, supplier terms, a deal,
a rate — and the user wants to walk in prepared rather than reactive.

## When NOT to use this

- A trivial, low-stakes exchange where preparation effort isn't worth
  it — just have the conversation.

## Workflow

1. **Separate positions from interests, for both sides.** A position is
   what someone asks for ("$50 a unit"); the interest is why they want
   it (cash-flow timing, margin protection, precedent for other deals).
   Interests are usually more flexible and more creative to solve for
   than the stated position.
2. **Define your BATNA** (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement) —
   what happens if this negotiation fails and you walk away. This is
   what actually sets your real power in the room, not how reasonable
   your ask feels.
3. **Estimate the other side's likely BATNA too**, even roughly — it
   tells you how much flexibility they actually have, independent of
   how firm they sound.
4. **Set a target and a genuine walk-away point** — the range you'd be
   happy with, and the point below (or above) which you actually leave
   the table. Decide this before the room, not during it.
5. **Look for multi-issue trades**, not just the one obvious number.
   Two parties fixated on a single price figure often miss value that's
   easy to unlock once timing, volume, term length, or other terms are
   brought into the mix.
6. **Plan your opening move and responses to common tactics** —
   anchoring, long silences, artificial deadlines — so they don't catch
   you improvising in the moment.

## Output format

```
## Negotiation prep: [what/who]

| | Your side | Their side |
|---|---|---|
| Stated position | ... | ... |
| Underlying interests | ... | ... |
| BATNA | ... | ... (estimate) |
| Target / walk-away range | ... | n/a |

### Possible multi-issue trades
[Beyond the headline number — timing, volume, term, other terms]

### Opening move & tactic responses
[Your first move, and how you'll handle anchoring/silence/deadline pressure]
```

## Common pitfalls

- Negotiating only on stated positions, never surfacing interests —
  leaves value on the table that both sides could have captured.
- No real BATNA, or an unrealistic one — makes any deal look better
  than it is, weakening your actual position.
- Fixating on a single issue instead of looking for trades across
  multiple terms.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Interests are identified separately from stated positions, both
      sides
- [ ] BATNA is realistic, not aspirational
- [ ] A genuine walk-away point is set before the negotiation, not
      during it
- [ ] At least one multi-issue trade is considered beyond the headline
      number
