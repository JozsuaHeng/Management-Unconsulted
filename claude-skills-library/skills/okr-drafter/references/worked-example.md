# Worked example: Q1 goal for Golden Island Cruises

Starting goal (as given, vague): "We want to grow the business and
improve customer experience next quarter."

### Turning it into OKRs

**Objective 1: Grow bookings sustainably without sacrificing service
quality**
- KR1: Increase monthly bookings from a baseline of [X] to [Y] by end of
  quarter
- KR2: Maintain average review rating at [current level] or above
  throughout the growth period (a guardrail key result — ensures growth
  isn't pursued at the cost of quality)
- KR3: Reduce average inquiry response time from [current] to [target]

**Objective 2: Validate the Gili route opportunity before committing
further investment**
- KR1: Complete the scouting trip and resolve any identified logistics
  issues
- KR2: Run and finalize unit economics for the route, with a clear
  go/no-go recommendation
- KR3: Conduct at least [N] discovery interviews with past multi-day
  customers about interest in the new route

### Why this is better than the original goal statement

"Grow the business" has no way to be marked done or not done. The
rewritten version:
- Splits into two Objectives so growth and the Gili route decision aren't
  conflated into one unmeasurable blob
- Each Key Result is a number with a clear before/after
- KR2 under Objective 1 is a **guardrail** — a deliberate check to ensure
  the push for growth (KR1) doesn't come at the cost of something equally
  important (service quality) — a useful pattern anytime one KR could be
  gamed at the expense of another

Note KR1-KR3 under Objective 2 are a mix of milestone-style key results
(complete the scouting trip) — acceptable when the Objective is genuinely
about *validating* something rather than a raw growth number, since
"validated with a clear answer" is itself a measurable, falsifiable
outcome even though it isn't a percentage.
