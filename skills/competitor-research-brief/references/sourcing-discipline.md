# Sourcing discipline for competitor research

## Good public sources, roughly in order of reliability for factual claims
1. The competitor's own website/booking platform (offering, stated
   pricing, stated positioning)
2. Business registries or tourism board listings (existence, scale,
   licensing where public)
3. Review platforms (TripAdvisor, Google Reviews) — good for volume/rating
   and recurring themes in feedback, less reliable for exact current
   pricing or offering details, which change faster than reviews update
4. News coverage or press releases (recent moves, expansion, notable
   changes)
5. Social media presence (positioning signals, recent activity level) —
   treat as directional, not authoritative

## What counts as "fact" vs. "estimate" vs. "assumption"

- **Fact**: directly observed from a named, current source (e.g. "Their
  website lists a 3-day package at $X as of [date], source: [URL]").
- **Estimate**: a calculated or inferred number based on stated facts
  (e.g. "Based on their listed fleet size of 3 boats and typical
  occupancy patterns, estimated monthly capacity is roughly Y trips").
- **Assumption**: a judgment call without direct backing (e.g. "Likely
  targeting a younger, budget-conscious segment based on pricing and
  marketing tone" — reasonable, but not confirmed).

## A note on verifying identity before starting

Before writing anything about an entity as a "competitor," check:
- Is this actually the user's own business or a sub-brand of it?
- Is this a booking agent/reseller rather than an operator — i.e. do they
  actually own/run the boats or service, or just sell someone else's?
- Could two differently-named listings be the same underlying operator?

Getting this wrong (researching your own business as a rival, or double-
counting one operator as two) undermines the whole brief, so it's worth
the extra minute up front.
