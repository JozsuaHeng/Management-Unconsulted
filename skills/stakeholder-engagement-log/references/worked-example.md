# Worked example: GIC Gili route stakeholder engagement log (excerpt)

| # | Stakeholder(s) | Date | Purpose | Discussion points | Outcome / follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Local dock operator (prospective partner) | [date] | Initial introduction and feasibility check | Dock capacity, mooring depth at low tide, indicative costs | Scouting trip scheduled to verify low-tide viability directly |
| 2 | Ops lead (internal) | [date] | Debrief after scouting trip | Low-tide mooring issue confirmed; alternative dock options discussed | Ops lead to shortlist 2 alternative docks by [date] |
| 3 | Local dock operator #2 (alternative) | [date] | Follow-up feasibility check | Availability, cost, low-tide depth confirmed viable | Move forward with this operator pending final agreement |

### Using the log operationally
Before entry #3's call, the log was checked — it showed exactly why the
first operator didn't work out (mooring depth at low tide), so the
second conversation could ask that question directly and early, rather
than repeating the same discovery process from scratch.

### Reviewing for gaps
If a high-influence stakeholder (e.g. the alternative dock operator)
hasn't appeared in the log for several weeks after an agreement was
expected, that gap itself is worth flagging and following up on — not
waiting for them to reach out first.
