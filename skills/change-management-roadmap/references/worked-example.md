# Worked example: GIC booking system rollout (booking staff)

| Stage | Activity | Audience | Timing | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Team meeting explaining why the spreadsheet is being replaced and the rough timeline | Booking staff | 4 weeks before go-live | Owner |
| Desire | 1:1 conversation addressing concerns (will this make my job harder before it gets easier?) and what's better about the new way | Booking staff | 3 weeks before | Owner |
| Knowledge | Hands-on training session on the new platform | Booking staff | 2 weeks before | Platform vendor / owner |
| Ability | Shadowed practice period — staff use the new system for real bookings with an experienced person available to help | Booking staff | Launch week | Owner (available on-call) |
| Reinforcement | Daily 10-minute check-in for the first week, then weekly for a month; explicitly ask "what's still going back to the old habit?" | Booking staff | Weeks 1-4 post-launch | Owner |

### Reinforcement plan
- Day 1-5: daily 10-minute check-in, fix friction immediately
- Week 2-4: weekly check-in, watch for reversion to the spreadsheet for
  "just this one booking"
- Week 6: confirm the old spreadsheet is genuinely no longer being used
  by anyone before considering the change complete

This is the stage a rushed rollout skips — without it, it's common for
one or two bookings a week to quietly go back into the old spreadsheet
"just this once," and within two months the old habit is fully back.
