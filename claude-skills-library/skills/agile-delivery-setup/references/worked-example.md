# Worked example: illustrative, generic small product team

### Roles
| Role | Held by | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| Product Owner | Product lead | Backlog priority, acceptance of finished work |
| Scrum Master | Delivery lead | Ceremony facilitation, removing blockers |
| Delivery team | 4 engineers | How the work gets built |

### Backlog
Definition of Ready: user story has a clear acceptance criterion,
no unresolved dependency on another team, and is estimated by the team.
Grooming cadence: weekly, 30 minutes, Product Owner + team.

### Sprint cadence
Sprint length: 2 weeks.

| Ceremony | Purpose | Cadence | Attendees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planning | Commit to sprint scope | Start of sprint, 1hr | Full team |
| Standup | Sync on progress/blockers | Daily, 10 min | Delivery team + SM |
| Review | Demo to stakeholders, gather feedback | End of sprint, 45 min | Team + stakeholders |
| Retro | Reflect on how the team worked | End of sprint, 30 min | Delivery team + SM |

### Definition of Done
Code reviewed by one other engineer, automated tests passing, deployed
to staging, and accepted by the Product Owner against the story's
stated acceptance criteria — not just "code complete."

Note the standup purpose is written as peer sync, not status reporting —
that framing is what keeps it from drifting into a manager check-in over
time, which is one of the most common ways Agile ceremonies decay.
