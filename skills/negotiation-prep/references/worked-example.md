# Worked example: GIC renegotiating fuel supplier terms

| | GIC (buyer) | Fuel supplier |
|---|---|---|
| Stated position | Wants a lower per-litre rate | Wants to hold current pricing |
| Underlying interests | Predictable cost for margin planning; doesn't necessarily need the lowest possible headline rate | Wants volume certainty and reliable payment timing more than the exact rate |
| BATNA | Could switch to Supplier B at a slightly higher rate but with less reliable delivery | Could lose the account, but GIC is a meaningful share of their local volume |
| Target / walk-away range | Target: 5% reduction. Walk-away: no worse than current rate with better payment terms | n/a |

### Possible multi-issue trades
Since the supplier's real interest is volume certainty and payment
timing more than the exact per-litre number, a 12-month volume
commitment in exchange for a smaller rate reduction plus better payment
terms (e.g. 30-day instead of 14-day) may satisfy both sides better than
purely fighting over the headline rate.

### Opening move & tactic responses
Open by naming the volume-commitment trade directly rather than just
asking for a discount — reframes the conversation around a trade instead
of a one-sided ask. If met with an anchored "we can't move on price,"
respond by moving the conversation to payment terms rather than
repeating the price ask.
