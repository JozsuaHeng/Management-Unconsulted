# Scoring rubric and cues for each force

Use these as reference cues when rating a force High / Medium / Low — not
a rigid formula, but a way to keep ratings honest and comparable across
analyses.

## Rivalry among existing competitors
- High: many similar-sized competitors, low differentiation, high fixed
  costs pushing everyone to compete on price, slow-growing market (fixed
  pie means share must be taken from someone).
- Low: few competitors, meaningful differentiation, growing market (room
  for everyone to grow without fighting over share).

## Bargaining power of buyers
- High: buyers are concentrated (a few big accounts), the product is a
  commodity to them, switching cost is low, buyers are price-sensitive and
  well-informed (e.g. compare on TripAdvisor/Google before booking).
- Low: buyers are fragmented (many small individual customers), product is
  differentiated in ways that matter to them, switching means real
  friction (e.g. lost deposit, unfamiliarity with a new operator).

## Bargaining power of suppliers
- High: few suppliers of a critical input (e.g. one fuel supplier, one
  boat builder in the region), switching suppliers is costly or slow,
  supplier could plausibly integrate forward into your business.
- Low: many interchangeable suppliers, low switching cost, input is not
  differentiated.

## Threat of new entrants
- High: low capital requirement, no licensing/regulatory barrier, no need
  for reputation or relationships to start selling, easy to copy the
  offering quickly.
- Low: high capital requirement (owning/chartering boats), licensing or
  permit requirements, reputation/trust takes years to build in a
  trip-booking category, access to key assets (good docks, experienced
  crew) is scarce.

## Threat of substitutes
- High: there's a genuinely different way to satisfy the same underlying
  need at comparable or lower cost/effort (a resort stay, a day-trip
  instead of overnight, a different destination entirely).
- Low: the experience being sold is distinctive enough that "something
  else" doesn't really scratch the same itch.

## Example: multi-day phinisi charters out of Lombok (illustrative, not
verified data — treat as a template to fill with real data)

| Force | Illustrative rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | Medium | Several operators, but real differentiation exists (boat quality, route, service level) — not a pure price fight yet. |
| Buyer power | Medium | Individual travelers, fragmented, but highly price/review-comparison-savvy before booking. |
| Supplier power | Low-Medium | Fuel and crew labor have some alternatives; dock access in specific locations can be a local bottleneck. |
| New entrants | Low-Medium | Capital and reputation-building are real barriers, but not prohibitive for a well-funded new entrant. |
| Substitutes | Medium | Resort stays and day-trips are real alternatives for some of the same travel budget. |

This table is a **template pattern**, not GIC's actual verified analysis —
any real use of this skill should replace these with sourced, current
findings, not reuse these illustrative numbers.
