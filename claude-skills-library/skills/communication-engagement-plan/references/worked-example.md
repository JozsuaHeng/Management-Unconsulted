# Worked example: GIC booking system change

| Audience | Key message | Channel | Sender | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Booking staff | What's changing, why, and what training looks like | Team meeting + follow-up doc | Owner | 4 weeks before |
| Crew/ops | Schedules will come from a new source but nothing changes for how they work day to day | Brief in-person mention at a regular crew meeting | Ops lead | 1 week before |
| Customers | Booking confirmations may look slightly different; nothing they need to do differently | Short email note alongside their next booking confirmation | Booking staff (as part of normal comms) | At go-live |

### Two-way engagement
Booking staff have a direct line to the owner for questions during the
transition (not a suggestion box that goes unanswered) — questions get a
same-day response during the first two weeks, since that's the period
uncertainty is highest.

### Timeline view
Announcement (why + timeline) → training detail (what/how) → go-live
support (day-of availability) → post-launch check-ins (per the
reinforcement plan in `change-management-roadmap`).

Note customers get almost no advance detail — correctly so, since the
change is nearly invisible to them and over-communicating to an
unaffected audience creates unnecessary anxiety about a "big change"
that isn't big for them.
