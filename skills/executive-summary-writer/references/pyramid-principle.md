# The Pyramid Principle, briefly

Developed by Barbara Minto at McKinsey, the core idea: structure written
communication top-down, not bottom-up. Most people naturally write in the
order they figured something out (background → analysis → conclusion).
Readers, especially busy executive readers, need it the other way around
(conclusion → supporting reasons → detail on request).

## The shape

```
                [Main message / answer]
               /          |          \
     [Reason 1]      [Reason 2]      [Reason 3]
       /    \           /    \          /    \
  [detail] [detail] [detail][detail] [detail][detail]
```

Each level answers "why?" or "so what?" for the level above it. A reader
should be able to stop reading at any level and still have gotten the
truth — just less detail.

## Grouping reasons (the "MECE at the reasons level" check)

The 3-5 supporting reasons should ideally be genuinely distinct
categories, not overlapping restatements. A quick test: could two of the
reasons be merged into one without losing anything? If yes, they weren't
really distinct reasons.

## Worked example

Bad (bottom-up, buries the point):
> "We looked at Q3 booking data across all channels. Marketing spend was
> reduced by 15% in August. Response times to inquiries also increased
> from 2 hours to 6 hours average. Additionally, our main competitor
> launched a new discounted package in July. Taking all this together,
> bookings for Q3 fell 22% versus Q2."

Good (Pyramid Principle, answer first):
> **Bottom line**: Q3 bookings fell 22% versus Q2, driven mainly by two
> controllable factors — reduced marketing spend and slower inquiry
> response times — not primarily by competitor activity.
>
> **Why**:
> - Marketing spend was cut 15% in August, reducing inquiry volume at the
>   top of the funnel.
> - Average inquiry response time rose from 2 hours to 6 hours, likely
>   reducing conversion of the inquiries that did come in.
> - A competitor's new discounted package launched in July, but timing
>   suggests it's a smaller contributor than the two factors above.
>
> **What this means**: Restoring response time to under 2 hours and
> reinstating August-level marketing spend are the two highest-leverage,
> controllable fixes for Q4.

Same facts, radically more useful because the reader gets the answer
immediately and the reasoning to trust it right after.
