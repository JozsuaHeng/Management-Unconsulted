# Question bank by objective

## Understanding why customers chose you (or didn't)
- Walk me through how you first found out about [business/offering].
- What were you trying to accomplish when you started looking?
- What other options did you consider?
- What tipped the decision toward [us]? (or, for lost prospects: what
  tipped it away?)
- Was there anything that almost made you not choose us?

## Understanding satisfaction / experience
- Walk me through your experience from booking to [end of experience].
- What stood out as better than expected? Worse than expected?
- If a friend asked you about this, what would you tell them?
- Was there a moment where you felt frustrated or unsure what to do?

## Understanding unmet needs / new offering validation
- Tell me about the last time you [relevant activity/need].
- What's the hardest part of that, currently?
- If you could wave a wand and fix one thing about [current approach],
  what would it be?
- (After describing a potential new offering) What's your honest
  reaction? What would need to be true for you to actually use this?

## A note on the last question above

Describing a new idea and asking "would you use this?" tends to get
overly polite, optimistic answers. Asking "what would need to be true for
you to actually use this" gets more honest, specific, actionable
information — people are better at stating real conditions than
predicting their own future behavior.

## Synthesis worked example

Say 6 customer interviews were done about a potential new multi-day Gili
route. Raw notes mention price concerns (4 of 6), worry about seasickness/
comfort on longer trips (3 of 6), and one mention of wanting a
kid-friendly option (1 of 6).

Theme 1: Price sensitivity for multi-day trips (4 of 6) — the dominant
theme, worth prioritizing in the business case.
Theme 2: Comfort/seasickness concern for longer trips (3 of 6) — a real
secondary theme, possibly addressable through marketing (route choice,
boat stability messaging) rather than requiring a product change.
Outlier: kid-friendly option interest (1 of 6) — noted, not yet a
pattern; worth watching in future interviews rather than acting on now.
