# Common act structures

Pick whichever fits the content — don't force one that doesn't match what
the deck is actually arguing.

## Situation-Complication-Question-Answer (SCQA)
Best for: decks that need to bring the reader along a reasoning path,
especially when the recommendation might be surprising or contested.
1. **Situation** — the stable context the reader already agrees with
2. **Complication** — what changed or what's the tension/problem
3. **Question** — the question this raises, made explicit
4. **Answer** — the recommendation, and the rest of the deck defends it

## Context-Problem-Options-Recommendation-Next steps
Best for: decision decks where multiple options were genuinely
considered and the reader needs to see the comparison, not just the pick.
1. **Context** — brief background
2. **Problem** — what needs deciding
3. **Options considered** — 2-4 real options, with tradeoffs shown
4. **Recommendation** — the pick, and why it beats the alternatives
5. **Next steps** — what happens after this deck is approved

## Findings-first (for research/analysis readouts)
Best for: sharing research or analysis results where there isn't yet a
single decision to make.
1. **Headline finding** — the single most important takeaway, up front
2. **Supporting findings** — 3-5 grouped findings, each with its own
   action-titled slide
3. **Implications** — what this means for the business
4. **Open questions** — what's still unknown, if relevant

## Worked example — one slide, done well

Bad: "Slide 4: Competitive Landscape" (topic label, no message)

Good: "Slide 4: Two competitors now offer multi-day packages, closing our
main differentiation gap" (action title — states the actual finding,
tells the reader what to take away without needing to read the chart)
Evidence needed: a simple comparison table (from
`competitive-landscape-mapper`) showing offering type across 3-4 named
competitors and GIC, dated.
