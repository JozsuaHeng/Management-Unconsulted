# Worked example: illustrative, generic system-replacement project

### Business case (one line)
Replace the legacy finance system to eliminate a 15,000-transaction
processing backlog and restore reliable financial reporting.

### Organisation
| Role | Held by | Accountable for |
|---|---|---|
| Executive | CFO | Overall business justification, final go/no-go |
| Senior User | Finance Operations Lead | Requirements represent real user needs |
| Senior Supplier | IT Delivery Lead | Solution is technically sound and deliverable |
| Project Manager | Program Manager | Day-to-day delivery within tolerances |

### Stage plan
| Stage | Deliverables | Time tolerance | Cost tolerance | Escalation trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stage 1: Stabilise | Critical defects resolved, backlog reduced 50% | ±1 week | ±5% | Backlog reduction under 30% at midpoint |
| Stage 2: Remediate | Core process fixes live | ±2 weeks | ±10% | Any Sev-1 defect introduced |
| Stage 3: Optimise | Reporting reliability restored, BAU handover | ±1 week | ±5% | Handover criteria not met |

### Reporting
Highlight report: fortnightly to the Board, one page, RAG status per
tolerance dimension. Exception report: raised immediately if any stage
tolerance is breached, with options and a recommendation — not just a
problem statement.

Note the escalation triggers are specific and measurable ("backlog
reduction under 30% at midpoint"), not vague ("if things go wrong") —
that specificity is what makes management-by-exception actually work
rather than defaulting to constant escalation.
