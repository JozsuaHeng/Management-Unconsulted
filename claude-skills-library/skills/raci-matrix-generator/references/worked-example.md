# Worked example: launching a new client proposal process

| Task/Decision | Responsible | Accountable | Consulted | Informed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Draft proposal content | Consultant | Consultant | Practice lead (on scope/pricing) | — |
| Approve final pricing | Practice lead | Practice lead | — | Consultant |
| Legal review of contract terms | External counsel | Practice lead | — | Consultant |
| Send proposal to client | Consultant | Consultant | — | Practice lead |
| Track proposal status/follow-up | Consultant | Consultant | — | Practice lead |

### Why this works
- Every row has exactly one Accountable person — even "Draft proposal
  content," where the consultant is both Responsible and Accountable,
  since it's their task end to end with no separate approver needed at
  the draft stage.
- "Approve final pricing" separates Responsible/Accountable (practice
  lead) from who did the drafting (consultant) — a deliberate check
  before anything goes to a client.
- Consulted is used sparingly — only where input genuinely changes the
  outcome (scope/pricing guidance, legal terms), not for every task.
- Informed is used to keep the practice lead aware of client-facing
  actions (sending, follow-up) without requiring their active
  involvement in each one.

### A common mistake this avoids
A weaker version might list "Practice lead" as Accountable for every
single row "to be safe" — this looks careful but actually creates a
bottleneck and blurs who's really doing and deciding what. RACI's value
comes from precision, not from defaulting to the most senior person
everywhere.
