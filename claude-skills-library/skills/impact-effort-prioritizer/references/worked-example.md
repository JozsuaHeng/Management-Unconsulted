# Worked example

Candidate initiatives for GIC (illustrative):
1. Fix the shared-calendar/booking-system redundancy (from the process
   map example)
2. Launch the Gili multi-day route
3. Redesign the website
4. Cross-train a second person on inquiry handling
5. Add a loyalty/repeat-customer discount program

| Initiative | Impact | Effort | Quadrant | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fix booking/accounting redundancy | Medium | Low | Do first | Removes ongoing error risk and delay, quick to implement |
| Cross-train inquiry backup | High | Low | Do first | Directly addresses a known response-time weakness (see root-cause example) cheaply |
| Loyalty/repeat-customer program | Medium | Medium | Plan carefully | Real upside, but needs design and tracking to do well |
| Gili route launch | High (uncertain — not yet validated) | High | Plan carefully, but validate first | Impact confidence is low until scouting trip + unit economics are done |
| Website redesign | Low-Medium | High | Don't do (for now) | No specific evidence current site is losing bookings; effort is high relative to unclear upside |

### Recommended sequence
1. Cross-train inquiry backup (quick win, addresses a known weakness)
2. Fix booking/accounting redundancy (quick win, low risk)
3. Validate the Gili route (small-cost scouting + unit economics, before
   committing to the full high-effort launch)
4. Design the loyalty program (worth planning properly, not urgent)

### Deprioritized
Website redesign — not ruled out forever, but deprioritized until there's
actual evidence (e.g. from unit economics, conversion data, or customer
interviews) that it's a real bottleneck rather than a guessed one.

This shows the key move: the Gili route isn't dismissed for being
high-effort, but its impact rating is flagged as uncertain, which changes
the recommendation from "do it big" to "validate cheaply first" — a
different action than either "do first" or "don't do."
