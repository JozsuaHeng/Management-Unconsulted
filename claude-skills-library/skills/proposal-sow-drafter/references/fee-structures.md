# Common fee structures, and when each fits

- **Fixed fee** — best when scope is well-defined and deliverables are
  concrete. Lowest client risk, highest provider risk if scope creeps
  (which is exactly why the "not included" list matters most here).
- **Time & materials (hourly/daily rate)** — best when scope is
  exploratory or likely to evolve. Lower provider risk, requires trust and
  transparent time tracking to avoid client concern about runaway hours.
- **Retainer** — best for ongoing advisory relationships rather than a
  single project with an end date; usually paired with a defined monthly
  scope of availability/hours rather than specific deliverables.
- **Value-based / success fee** — tied to an outcome (e.g. % of cost
  savings identified). Higher potential upside, but requires a very
  clearly measurable outcome and trust in how it's measured — riskiest to
  set up well, and usually needs the most explicit definition of how
  success is measured and attributed.

## Change-order clause, example language

"Any request that materially changes scope, deliverables, or timeline
from what's described above will be scoped and quoted separately before
work begins on it. Minor clarifications within the original scope are
included at no extra charge."

This kind of clause protects the relationship as much as the fee — it
gives a neutral, pre-agreed process for scope changes instead of an
awkward renegotiation once something unexpected comes up mid-engagement.

## Assumptions/dependencies — common ones worth stating explicitly

- Timely access to requested data or documents
- Availability of key stakeholders for interviews/decisions within the
  proposed timeline
- Feedback turnaround time on draft deliverables (state an expected
  window, e.g. "5 business days")
- Any third-party tools/systems access needed to complete the work
