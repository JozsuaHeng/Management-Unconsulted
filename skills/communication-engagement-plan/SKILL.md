# Communication & Engagement Plan

## When to use this

A change, program, or initiative needs to be communicated to more than
one audience, and a single blanket announcement won't serve everyone's
actual information needs.

## When NOT to use this

- One audience, one simple message — just write the message directly.
- The plan is really about the fuller change-adoption picture (training,
  reinforcement, not just messaging) — use `change-management-roadmap`,
  which this plan feeds into.

## Workflow

1. **Segment the audience** — ideally from a `change-impact-assessment`
   or `stakeholder-influence-map` — since different groups need
   different information, not the same message repeated.
2. **For each segment, define what they specifically need to know and
   by when** — a booking-staff audience needs process detail; a
   customer audience needs only "what changes for you," not internal
   process detail.
3. **Match channel to audience**, not one channel for everyone — a
   team meeting works for staff, an email works for customers, a notice
   board works for on-site crew who don't check email often.
4. **Sequence messages across the timeline** — don't front-load every
   detail at first announcement; build understanding progressively
   (what's changing → why → what it means for you → how to get ready).
5. **Choose the right sender for credibility** — some messages land
   better from a direct manager than from a general company
   announcement; note who should deliver each message, not just what
   it says.
6. **Build in two-way engagement, not just broadcast** — a channel for
   questions/feedback, and a plan to actually respond to it. One-way
   communication that never acknowledges questions erodes trust fast.

## Output format

```
## Communication & engagement plan: [change/program]

| Audience | Key message | Channel | Sender | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

### Two-way engagement
[How feedback/questions get captured and who responds, and by when]

### Timeline view
[Simple sequence: announcement → detail → readiness support → go-live →
post-launch]
```

## Common pitfalls

- One message for every audience regardless of what they actually need
  to know.
- Front-loading all detail at the first announcement instead of
  building understanding progressively.
- No feedback channel, or a feedback channel that's never actually
  monitored/responded to.
- Choosing convenience of channel over what the audience actually reads
  (e.g. email to a crew that doesn't check it regularly).

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Messages are tailored per audience, not copy-pasted
- [ ] Channel and sender are chosen for the specific audience, not
      defaulted
- [ ] Messages are sequenced across time, not delivered all at once
- [ ] A real two-way feedback mechanism exists and has an owner
